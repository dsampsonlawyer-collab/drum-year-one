Look after your hearing and hands
›
  • A kit is loud enough to damage hearing over time. Wear earplugs made for music, or use a practice pad or electronic kit for most sessions.
  • Warm up for 3 minutes before every session: slow singles and doubles on the pad.
  • Grip loosely. The stick should bounce back by itself. If you squeeze, your hands will hurt and your speed will stall.
  • Stop and rest if you feel sharp pain, numbness or tingling. Tired muscles are normal. Pain is not.
  • Take a short break every 20-30 minutes.
Kit setup checklist
›
  • Throne height: thighs slope slightly down, feet flat or on the pedals with no strain.
  • Snare at about belly-button height, tilted slightly toward you.
  • Hi-hat just left of the snare, kick pedal under your right foot, cymbals within an easy reach without stretching.
  • Cymbal wing nuts loose so the cymbal can swing when you hit it.
  • Metronome ready, phone ready for recording, practice pad and sticks within reach.
A 15-minute practice session
›
  • 3 min: warm-up. Slow singles, doubles and paradiddles at a comfortable tempo.
  • 4 min: this week's rudiment or reading exercise, with the metronome.
  • 5 min: this week's groove and fill. Start 10 BPM under the target.
  • 3 min: play along to a track or this week's backing track.

Slow is fast. If you can not play it cleanly three times in a row, slow down. Record yourself once a week and listen back. Use the Rudiments tab to check your timing.

Reading the lessons
›
  • x is a hit, . is a rest, o is a snare or kick hit, g is a ghost note (very soft), O is an open hi-hat.
  • A line like 1 e & a 2 e & a is the count. Say it out loud as you play.
  • Capital R or L in a sticking pattern is an accent (louder). Lowercase is a soft tap.
  • The tests are not a pass or fail grade. They show what to practise next. Retest as often as you like.
